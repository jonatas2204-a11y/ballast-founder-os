# Decision gates

A high numerical score is not enough when an unresolved unknown could invalidate the business model.

## Financial-partner hard gates

Do not recommend a partner as launch-ready until these are confirmed:

1. **Distribution architecture** — can it legally and operationally support Ballast → merchant → end-borrower?
2. **Funding** — who provides principal and under what limits/conditions?
3. **Credit risk** — who bears default loss and what recourse/guarantees exist?
4. **Origination/contracting** — which regulated entity is creditor/originator and signs the borrower contract?
5. **Servicing and collections** — who handles statements, repayment, delinquency, renegotiation and complaints?
6. **Regulatory role allocation** — what registrations, correspondent arrangements or regulated obligations apply to Ballast and the merchant?
7. **Data/LGPD** — controller/operator roles, consent/legal basis, data retention, portability and incident allocation.
8. **Economics** — every fee, revenue share, minimum, reserve, subsidy and clawback that can materially affect Ballast economics.
9. **Pilot feasibility** — product, ticket range, geography, integration method and realistic implementation path.

Any gate marked `UNKNOWN` means: promising but not decision-ready.

## Merchant pilot gates

Do not call a merchant pilot-ready until:

- the decision maker has authority to test;
- typical ticket and financing use case are understood;
- customer journey can insert financing without breaking the sales process;
- merchant economics and any subsidy are explicit;
- operational owner is named;
- customer support escalation is defined;
- measurable pilot success criteria exist.

## Evidence hierarchy

`VERIFIED_PUBLIC` and `USER_PROVIDED` may close a gate if the evidence is direct enough. `ESTIMATE` and `ASSUMPTION` never close a hard gate. `UNKNOWN` is an explicit due-diligence item.
