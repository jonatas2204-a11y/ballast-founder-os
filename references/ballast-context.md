# Ballast context

Ballast is developing a B2B2C distribution model for embedded consumer credit in Brazil, initially oriented toward high-ticket purchases and projects in the architecture, interiors and construction ecosystem.

The preferred model is:

1. A merchant or service provider presents a financing option to its end-customer.
2. Ballast provides the commercial/distribution layer, integration and partner relationship.
3. A regulated financial partner performs or owns the required regulated activities such as credit decisioning, origination and contracting.
4. Funding and credit risk should preferably not require Ballast to deploy its own balance sheet.
5. The operating model may support merchant-specific branding or a white-label experience, subject to partner and regulatory constraints.

Treat these as strategic preferences, not facts about any specific partner.

## Initial merchant focus

High-ticket segments where financing can preserve customer liquidity, accelerate purchase decisions or improve conversion without positioning the customer as unable to pay:

- planned furniture and custom cabinetry;
- premium building materials and finishes;
- kitchens and wardrobes;
- flooring and coverings;
- stone, countertops and surfaces;
- windows, glass and frames;
- lighting;
- home automation;
- HVAC and residential climate systems;
- pools and landscaping;
- solar and other home-capex categories;
- architecture/interior-design-linked projects where a finance layer can support execution.

Initial geography: Greater São Paulo, with emphasis on class A/B consumers and merchants with enough volume for a repeatable B2B2C channel.

## Strategic objective

Build a scalable distribution layer where Ballast can:

- acquire and activate merchants;
- embed a financing journey into merchant sales;
- route eligible customers to one or more financial partners;
- earn transparent commercial economics without taking unsupported regulatory or credit risk;
- learn which merchant segments, partner structures and credit products generate the best risk-adjusted contribution.
