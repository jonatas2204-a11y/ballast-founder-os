# Ballast state

Last updated: 2026-10-11

## Thesis
Ballast is building a B2B2C embedded-credit distribution layer for high-ticket purchases and projects, initially focused on the architecture, interiors and construction ecosystem. The preferred model is for Ballast to own merchant distribution and the commercial layer while a regulated financial partner handles credit underwriting, origination/contracting, funding and the regulated servicing stack as applicable.

## Current stage
`PARTNER DILIGENCE + MERCHANT DISCOVERY`

The business is still validating the financial-partner architecture and prospecting the first high-fit merchant segments. No claim of validated product-market fit is recorded.

## Target merchant wedge
Initial focus:
- planned furniture and custom cabinetry;
- premium building materials and finishes;
- interior-design-linked purchases;
- high-ticket home and construction categories where financing can preserve liquidity or accelerate a project.

## Geography
Initial commercial wedge: Greater São Paulo.

## Desired credit architecture
Preferred structure:
`consumer → merchant → Ballast → regulated financial partner`

Preferred characteristics:
- Ballast does not deploy its own balance sheet to fund loans;
- partner performs underwriting and regulated origination/contracting;
- partner or its funding structure supplies principal;
- credit-risk allocation is explicit and should not be silently shifted to Ballast;
- white-label / merchant-branded journey is desirable where legally and operationally viable;
- API or a robust hosted journey is desirable;
- servicing, collections, complaints and LGPD responsibilities are explicitly allocated.

## Financial partners
Current candidates discussed or under investigation:
- Open Co / Geru — CaaS / embedded-finance fit under diligence.
- Grupo 3SA / possible related white-label fintech referred to as “Credaxx” or similar — identity and capabilities require verification before inclusion as a qualified partner.
- BYX — known from prior outreach as a provider relevant to payroll-deducted credit; current fit for Ballast's broader use case appears limited and requires product-specific evaluation.

No partner is marked launch-ready yet.

## Known economics
`UNKNOWN / NOT YET VALIDATED`

Inputs that still need reliable evidence include:

## Evidence inventory
Available evidence classes:

- prior outreach/contact findings;
- no production loan-performance dataset recorded yet;
- no funded Ballast pilot dataset recorded yet.

## Decisions made
1. Ballast should prioritize a B2B2C embedded-credit architecture rather than becoming the funding balance sheet itself in the initial model.
2. Greater São Paulo is the initial merchant-prospecting geography.
3. High-ticket architecture/interiors/construction merchants are the initial commercial wedge.
4. Partner diligence must verify funding, risk allocation, regulated roles, servicing and multi-layer distribution before commercial commitment.
5. Merchant prospecting must not treat absence of a public financing page as proof that the merchant has no financing.

## Active experiments
- Financial-partner research and outreach.
- Ranking high-potential merchants in Greater São Paulo.
- Testing outreach language and account-specific prospecting.

## Highest-risk unknowns
1. Which financial partner can actually support Ballast → merchant → end-customer distribution at acceptable economics?
2. Who bears credit risk and provides funding under the target partnership?
3. What regulatory/correspondent obligations would apply to Ballast and merchant partners?
4. Which merchant vertical produces the best combination of ticket, financing demand, decision access and repeatable volume?
5. What merchant economics are strong enough to support Ballast revenue without hurting merchant conversion or margin?
6. What borrower product terms will fit the target projects while remaining commercially competitive?

## Next three decisions
1. Select 1–2 financial partners for formal diligence / pilot negotiation.
2. Select the first merchant vertical and 10–20 target accounts for structured discovery.
3. Define a pilot economics model and success gates before implementation.
