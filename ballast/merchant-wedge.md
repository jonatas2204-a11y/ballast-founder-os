# Initial merchant wedge

Last updated: 2026-10-11

## Priority thesis
Ballast should initially test merchants where financed purchases are large enough that card limits or cash liquidity can constrain timing, but the customer may still have strong income and repayment capacity.

Financing should be positioned as a way to preserve liquidity, execute a project sooner or spread a capital-intensive home purchase—not as a signal that the consumer cannot afford the project.

## Segments to rank first
1. Planned furniture / custom cabinetry.
2. Premium kitchens and wardrobes.
3. Premium finishes, flooring and coverings.
4. Stone, surfaces and countertops.
5. Windows, glass and frames.
6. Lighting and home automation.
7. HVAC / residential climate systems.
8. Pools and landscaping.
9. Solar / residential capex where merchant underwriting fit is acceptable.
10. Architecture/interior-design firms with meaningful project procurement or execution flows.

## Merchant qualification dimensions
- ticket / financing need;
- class A/B fit;
- volume / scale;
- decision-maker access;
- financing gap or dissatisfaction with current alternatives;
- operational fit for a financing journey.

Use `scripts/lead_score.py` only after evidence exists for the score.

## Discovery questions
- What is the typical project/order ticket?
- What payment structures are used today?
- How often does a client ask to extend payment beyond current card/installment options?
- Are projects delayed, downsized or lost because of payment structure?
- Do you currently refer customers to a bank/fintech or offer any dedicated financing?
- Who owns the commercial decision to test a new financing option?
- How would financing need to fit the sales process to avoid slowing the showroom/project journey?
- What would make a 60–90 day pilot worth continuing?
